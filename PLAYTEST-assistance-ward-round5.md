## 6. Difficulty and fairness record

**Area 1 — goal, obstacle and available tools.** All three of Bycer's questions were answerable without notes: the register and AI verdict give the goal (“The entry stands until mastery of the whole of Unit 1 is demonstrated. You will demonstrate it here.”), the recovery board and blocked dial give the five-part obstacle (“All five records are required.”), and the four pupils, practice props and AI audit are visible tools. Every gate had prose plus a physical label or plaque.

**Area 2 — clue discoverability and repeated progress.** Time to first meaningful insight: from New Game (14:07) to Nia's chipped corner (about 14:08) took roughly one minute in ordinary play. The final ward puzzle was understood when the board's five labels were read and solved when the dial confirmed all five sockets lit. Repeated actions with no new information: `examine recovery board` repeated the five labels after each recovery; the dial block repeated its socket list twice; the deliberate wrong quiz answers produced explanations that changed the next attempt. Both N6 clues were found for each gate through ordinary exploration.

**Area 3 — reasonable commands rather than one exact phrase.** Spaced `talk to`, hyphenated `talk-to`, written `PLACE`, `put`, `take`/`get practice block`, both item-first unlock forms, Priya's own-words reason and all four arrival EXAMINE synonyms worked. The wrong-order unlock and the fixed bell/cabinet produced in-world reasons rather than bare refusals. All three taught quiz answer forms worked, so no essential action depended on one exact phrase.

**Area 4 — hints.** Level 1 in each of the three ward rooms, naming the stalled object or half-step without supplying an answer; no score penalty and no automatic offer. After a hint the next action was clear (for example, “Say which one is holding you up before you act.”). The two-turn cooldown was confirmed in earlier rounds; this round re-confirmed the room mapping and the lack of unprompted offers.

**Area 5 — quiz readability, consequences and explanations.** Four questions were shown with numbered options in a dedicated panel; the three-of-four pass, zero score, no life loss, no movement and unlimited retries were stated before question 1. Every answer, right or wrong, got a specific reason including why the distractors were wrong. The deliberate 2/4 fail left the pupil in Records at score 0, lives 3 with an immediate retry at “Question 1 of 4”. The unlock explanation named both working forms, so a correct answer could be copied into the room. The exact-option-text form is now genuinely usable, which removes the last avoidable wording trap in the ward.

**Area 6 — failure preservation, repeated progress and GCSE suitability.** No ward failure removed a record or a life, moved the pupil or forced a repeated walk. Ward lives stayed at 3 and score at 0 from New Game to completion, including save/reload after placing the block. The placement loop now rewards the intermediate step with a clear message, an updated room description and a state-aware item description, so a pupil can confirm the action without guessing. The reasoning involves observation, evidence, command form and a five-part sequence, which suits a GCSE learner; difficulty comes from interpretation rather than dense prose or punishment. After a hint or failure the player could say what to try next and do it: the dial named “ORIENTATION PASS needs the practice quiz”, the quiz explanations named the rules, Priya asked for the reason in the pupil's own words, and the block's own description said “no ward route depends on keeping it”. Abandonment point: none in the ward.
